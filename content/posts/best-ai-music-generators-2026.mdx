Most AI music tools tell you the same things in their marketing: fast, easy, royalty-free. What they don't tell you clearly is when the music you generate is actually yours to use commercially, and when it isn't.

The difference matters. Some tools give you commercial rights at $6/month. Others charge $30. One lets you monetize tracks on YouTube while keeping copyright for themselves. One has no free plan at all, even though the interface lets you browse endlessly.

I went through six services — not audio quality reviews, but the practical question: what do the free plans actually include, and what's the real price for legitimate commercial use?

## Suno

Suno is the most widely used text-to-music generator right now. You describe what you want — genre, mood, tempo, lyrical themes — and it generates a full song with vocals in under a minute.

**Free plan:** 50 credits per day, roughly 10 song generations. No commercial rights — personal use only.

**Pro plan ($10/month, $8/month billed annually):** Commercial rights included. You can distribute on Spotify, YouTube, and other platforms and keep the revenue.

**Premier plan ($30/month, $24/month billed annually):** 10,000 credits per month vs 2,500 on Pro, expanded commercial rights.

One detail that catches people out: tracks generated on the free plan don't gain commercial rights when you upgrade. Rights apply from the point of subscription, not retroactively. If you're building a catalog you plan to release, subscribe before you generate.

Suno has been navigating legal pressure from major labels — settlements with Warner (November 2025) and ongoing proceedings with UMG and Sony. As of writing, Pro subscribers retain commercial rights to what they generate, but this is a space where re-reading the current terms before commercial use is worth a few minutes. There's also a [download limit taking effect September 3, 2026](/blog/suno-september-2026-changes-explained) that's worth knowing about separately.

**Best for:** songwriters who want full songs with vocals, musicians who want to hear ideas quickly, content creators at $10/month.

## Udio

Udio generates music in a similar way to Suno — text prompt in, full track out. The audio quality is comparable and some people prefer it for specific genres. The pricing structure is where things diverge significantly.

**Free plan:** 10 credits per day, with a monthly fallback of 100 credits. Up to 3 songs per day. No commercial rights.

**Standard plan ($10/month):** 2,400 credits per month. This is where people get surprised: **the Standard plan does not include commercial rights**. This is the same price as Suno Pro, which does include commercial rights.

**Pro plan ($30/month):** 6,000 credits per month. Commercial rights are included here.

If you're comparing Suno and Udio purely on price of entry for commercial use: Suno gets you there at $10/month, Udio at $30/month. That's a meaningful difference depending on how much you actually generate each month.

Udio has its own legal complications — settlements with Universal Music Group (October 2025) and Warner (November 2025) are on record. The download policy has been in flux. Commercial rights exist on the Pro plan, but the context of ongoing proceedings is relevant if you're planning to distribute seriously.

**Best for:** producers who want more control over output style and are comfortable paying $30/month for commercial use.

## ElevenLabs Music

ElevenLabs is primarily known for voice synthesis, but its Music product has become a serious option for AI-generated tracks. The main distinction from Suno and Udio: ElevenLabs states its model was trained on licensed data through partnerships with Merlin Network and Kobalt Music Group.

What that means practically: the training data claim is documented. For the commercial use case, that gives this tool a different footing than services where training data sourcing is still in dispute.

**Free plan:** You can access the generator and hear output, but no commercial license. Testing only.

**Starter plan ($6/month):** Commercial rights are included — the lowest entry point of any service in this comparison. Important caveat: self-serve plans (Starter through Scale) explicitly exclude commercial use in **film, TV, and studio games**. For those categories, you'd need an Enterprise agreement.

**Creator ($22/month) / Pro ($99/month) / Scale ($299/month):** Each tier increases output limits and features with the same self-serve commercial rights and the same exclusions for film/TV/studio games.

**Enterprise:** Full commercial coverage without those exclusions.

If you're making music for YouTube, Spotify, podcasts, social media, or ad sync that isn't studio games or film/TV — Starter at $6/month is a real option. If you're working in those excluded categories, that's a meaningful limitation to know about before committing.

**Best for:** content creators who want a licensed-training argument behind their music, at the lowest commercial price point in this list.

## Stable Audio

Stable Audio is Stability AI's music generator — the same company behind Stable Diffusion. The tool generates audio from text prompts with a focus on instrumental music and sound design rather than full songs with vocals.

This is a more technically-oriented tool than Suno or Udio. You'll get better results here if you think in terms of BPM, instrumentation, and genre specifics rather than song concepts. It's not built for "generate me a pop song" — it's built for "generate a 120 BPM dark electronic loop."

**Free plan:** 20 tracks per month, 45-second maximum per track. No commercial rights. The length cap makes this usable for short-form content but genuinely limited for most other purposes.

**Pro plan ($12/month):** 500 tracks per month, up to 3 minutes per track, commercial rights included.

**Max plan ($36/month):** 2,000 tracks per month, same commercial terms as Pro.

If you need instrumental loops, ambient textures, or sound design elements — and want clean, verifiable commercial terms at $12/month — Stable Audio is a solid option that doesn't come with the legal-proceedings backdrop of the top two tools in this list.

**Best for:** producers, game developers, and content creators who need instrumental music and don't need vocals.

## AIVA

AIVA is one of the older AI composition tools, and its focus is different from everything else in this comparison: it's built for orchestral, cinematic, and classical music. Full string arrangements, film score aesthetics, solo piano compositions — AIVA is doing something the newer vocal generators aren't designed for.

The commercial rights structure has a distinction worth spelling out clearly.

**Free plan:** 3 compositions per month, MP3 only. The license is Creative Commons non-commercial — you can listen and share, but you can't monetize, and you have to credit AIVA as the composer.

**Standard plan (~$11/month):** 15 downloads per month, WAV export available. At this tier, you can monetize on YouTube, Twitch, TikTok, and Instagram. **But AIVA retains the copyright.** You can put the music in your videos and earn AdSense revenue, but the composition legally belongs to AIVA.

**Pro plan (~$33/month):** Unlimited downloads. At this tier, **you own the copyright**. That's the meaningful difference between Standard and Pro — not just more downloads, but actual legal ownership of what you generate. Pro also includes MIDI export and custom style profiles.

Practical consequence: if you're distributing on Spotify or Apple Music, you need the Pro plan. Distributing tracks where copyright belongs to AIVA would put you in a complicated position. If you're making music for YouTube content where ad revenue is the goal — Standard works, because you're monetizing the video, not registering the composition.

**Best for:** composers who want orchestral and cinematic output; people who specifically need a tool with explicit, well-defined licensing terms.

## Soundraw

Soundraw takes a different approach than everything else here. Instead of generating from a text description of a song, Soundraw offers a library of AI-generated tracks organized by mood, genre, and energy — and lets you customize them in a browser editor (adjust structure, tempo, instrumentation).

It's closer to working with a royalty-free music library than a generative AI tool. The use case is content creation, not music composition.

**Free plan:** Soundraw lets you browse and edit tracks without signing up, but you cannot download anything without a subscription. This is effectively an extended preview, not a free plan.

**Creator plan ($11.04/month):** Unlimited MP3 downloads, commercial use for videos, podcasts, advertisements, and client work.

**Artist Starter plan ($19.49/month):** 10 downloads per month. This tier adds the ability to distribute on streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, etc.).

**Artist Pro plan ($23.39/month):** 20 downloads per month, WAV files and stems included.

One important limitation across all plans: you cannot register Soundraw tracks with YouTube Content ID or similar rights management systems. The license is shared — the same track can be downloaded by many subscribers — so exclusive registration isn't possible. This is worth knowing if Content ID monetization is part of your plan. For general content use (ads, video, podcasts), this doesn't matter; for claiming tracks as your own exclusive content, it does.

**Best for:** video creators, podcasters, and ad agencies who want customizable royalty-free music without making full songs from scratch.

## How to choose based on what you're doing

**Lowest price for commercial use:** ElevenLabs Starter at $6/month, if you're not working in film/TV/studio games.

**Full songs with vocals for streaming distribution:** Suno Pro at $10/month is the most affordable path. Udio requires $30/month for the same — worth knowing before you pick one.

**Orchestral or cinematic music:** AIVA is the only tool in this list built for that. Standard ($11/month) covers YouTube monetization; Pro ($33/month) is what you need for streaming distribution and copyright ownership.

**Instrumental music for production use:** Stable Audio at $12/month has clean terms and no ongoing legal proceedings in the background.

**Music for video content, not distribution:** Soundraw Creator at $11/month is designed for that use case and works cleanly for it — just don't expect to release those tracks on Spotify.

These prices and terms are current as of August 2026. This is a space where policies are moving quickly — check each service's pricing page and terms of service before subscribing.
