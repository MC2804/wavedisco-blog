*By Serj · September 2026 · 7 min read*

There is a specific moment that many Suno users recognize: you generate a track, the music sounds good, but the vocals feel slightly off. Not wrong enough to delete — but not quite right either. A harshness on the S sounds. A metallic edge on long notes. Something that marks the voice as generated rather than recorded.

This article covers what those artifacts actually are, what post-processing addresses, and what it cannot. I'll separate the two clearly, because most guides on this topic oversell what's possible.

---

## What makes Suno vocals sound like AI

A few characteristics appear consistently:

**Harsh sibilance.** The S, SH, and T sounds often carry an aggressive edge — a bright, sometimes buzzy quality in the upper frequencies. This is one of the most recognizable AI vocal artifacts and one of the easier ones to reduce.

**Metallic texture on sustained notes.** When a vocal holds a pitch for more than a second, there's sometimes a quality that experienced listeners call "digital" — a subtle thinness in the tone that doesn't sound quite like a human voice sustaining a note.

**Uneven word-to-word dynamics.** Some words hit louder than others in ways that feel random rather than expressive. Human singers shape phrases with intention; Suno's dynamics within a phrase can be inconsistent.

**Hot output that affects the vocal.** Suno exports at loud levels. The internal loudness processing affects the vocal too, which can make it feel squashed in a way that doesn't always serve the song. More on that: [Why Does My Suno Track Sound Worse After I Download It?](/blog/why-suno-song-sounds-worse-after-download)

## The distinction that matters: stems or no stems

Before anything else: **Suno's vocal is baked into the mix by default.** You get one file containing everything — drums, instruments, and vocals together.

If you process the full mix, every adjustment aimed at the vocal also hits the instruments. A de-esser that softens the harsh S sounds will also soften the cymbals. A cut in the harsh frequency range affects everything in that range.

**On Suno's Pro plan**, you can download stems separately: vocal and instrumental as separate files, via the three-dot menu on any track → *Get stems / MIDI*. Processing an isolated vocal stem gives you significantly more control. Both paths are covered below.

---

## Path 1: Processing the full mix (no stems)

If you're on the free plan or prefer not to use stems, you can still reduce the most obvious vocal artifacts through whole-mix processing.

**In WaveDisco Studio — Manual Mode** ([full guide here](/blog/wavedisco-manual-mode)):

Find the **VOICE FIX** section. It's designed specifically for vocal artifacts in AI-generated music.

**EQ-Metal** — enable this first. It targets the metallic, digital character on sustained notes. The effect is subtle on some tracks and clearly audible on others; worth enabling by default and listening.

**De-Sibilance** — this is the de-esser. Set **THRESH** so it catches the sharpest S sounds without pulling down ordinary consonants. **MAX CUT** controls the reduction depth. Start conservatively — you want to soften the harshness, not remove consonants entirely. Check that hi-hats and cymbals haven't gone dull in the same pass; on a full mix, they're in the same frequency range and will be affected.

**Body** — adds warmth in the lower-mid range. Useful if the voice sounds thin. Be careful if there's already substantial bass in the track; this can add muddiness.

**Clarity** — adds presence. Use if the voice sits too far back in the mix after de-essing.

After VOICE FIX adjustments, run the **Limiter** with ceiling at -1 dBFS (True Peak) before export. The hot Suno output needs this for any streaming upload.

**The realistic limit:** you're processing everything together. The improvement to the vocal comes with trade-offs on the instruments. That's the honest constraint of working without stems.

---

## Path 2: Vocal stem processing (Pro plan)

With stems, you're working on the vocal in isolation. The results are more controlled and the trade-offs disappear.

**Step 1: Download stems from Suno**

In your library, open the three-dot menu on the track → *Get stems / MIDI*. You'll receive a vocal stem and an instrumental stem as separate files. This is a Pro plan feature.

**Step 2: Process the vocal stem in WaveDisco Manual Mode**

Upload only the vocal stem. Every VOICE FIX adjustment now affects only the voice:

- **De-Sibilance**: you can push MAX CUT further than on a full mix without worrying about cymbals. Focus on the harshest moments — the THRESH setting determines which sounds it catches.
- **EQ-Metal**: addresses the digital texture on sustained notes.
- **Body**: adds warmth. Without the full instrumental present, there's more room to boost without muddying the low end.
- **Compressor**: even out the word-to-word dynamic inconsistencies. Keep the ratio moderate — heavy compression on an AI vocal can make the dynamics feel more robotic, not less.

Export the processed vocal at 24-bit WAV.

**Step 3: Recombine with the instrumental**

Import both the processed vocal stem and the original instrumental stem into any audio editor. Audacity is free and handles this directly: import both tracks, align them from the start, and export as a single stereo file. You now have a track where the vocal was addressed independently of the instrumentation.

## What post-processing cannot fix

**Wrong words or mispronunciations.** If Suno sang something you didn't intend, no processing changes it. Regenerate.

**Unnatural phrasing.** If the vocal rhythm sounds like someone reading from a script — emphasis on wrong syllables, line breaks that don't feel human — this comes from the generation stage. Post-processing affects tone, not timing.

**A vocal style that doesn't match the track.** If the generation produced a style that doesn't fit the mood you wanted, the answer is a better prompt or a new generation.

**Fundamental pitch problems on whole phrases.** Pitch correction software can help with some issues, but requires stems and some technical familiarity. And correcting the pitch of an AI vocal too aggressively can make it sound more synthetic, not less.

---

## The short version

Most of what makes Suno vocals feel artificial is set at generation time. Post-processing removes specific technical artifacts — harshness, metallic texture, uneven loudness — and it does that job well. The VOICE FIX section in WaveDisco Manual Mode handles the main ones. Stems on the Pro plan make the work more precise.

What post-processing doesn't do is convert AI-generated vocals into something that sounds like a studio recording. That's not a failure of the tools — it's just an honest description of what they're for.

If the vocal in a generation bothers you beyond what processing can address, the most efficient answer is usually to regenerate with a more specific style prompt, then process the technical artifacts out of the better take.
