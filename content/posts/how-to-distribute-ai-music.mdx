You made an AI track in Suno or Treblo. It sounds good. Now you want it on Spotify. And then you open a browser and start reading distributor policies and the whole thing gets complicated fast.

Here is what I found when I went through this myself. Which distributors actually accept AI-generated music, which ones don't, and what you need to do before you upload.

## The first honest thing to know

Not everyone takes AI music. And the ones that do have conditions. The landscape shifted a lot in 2025–2026, mostly in response to pressure from labels and rights holders. Policies are still changing — what I describe here reflects the situation as of mid-2026, and it's worth checking each distributor's current terms before you upload anything.

## Who accepts AI music and who doesn't

| Distributor | AI Music | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **DistroKid** | ✅ Yes | Disclosure required, paid subscription needed for commercial rights on Suno/Treblo |
| **TuneCore** | ⚠️ Partially | Rejects "100% AI-generated" — requires human creative input in composition or performance |
| **CD Baby** | ❌ No | Full ban since October 2025 — AI mastering tools are fine, AI composition is not |
| **Amuse** | ✅ Yes | Accepts AI music, worth checking current tier requirements |

The short version: **DistroKid is currently the clearest path** for tracks generated with Suno or Treblo. They explicitly accept AI music, have a defined disclosure process, and work with the major AI generators.

## What DistroKid actually requires

DistroKid's process includes a disclosure step during upload where you indicate whether AI was involved in generating the audio. This is not optional — failing to disclose can result in removal later. Their system actively scans uploads and re-scans existing catalogs, so tracks that weren't flagged initially can be flagged retroactively if detected.

What they check:
- Did AI generate any part of the audio? (Disclose it)
- Does the artist identity represent a real person or an AI persona?
- Is this a one-off release or mass-generated spam? (Spam uploads get pulled)

**On commercial rights:** DistroKid requires that you have commercial rights to the music you upload. For Suno, that typically means being on a paid subscription at the time the track was generated — the free tier on Suno generally restricts commercial use. For Treblo, the Terms of Service (Section 8, as of June 2026) assigns output rights to the creator on any tier, including free. Check the current terms on both platforms before uploading — policies can and do change.

## What TuneCore means by "human creative input"

TuneCore's position is that they accept music where AI was used as a tool — but not music where AI was the sole creator. In practice, this means if you wrote the lyrics, sang the vocals, and used AI to generate an instrumental backing, that may qualify. If you typed a prompt into Suno and downloaded the result, that likely does not.

Their guidelines also include requirements around confirming that the AI model was trained on authorized data — which is genuinely difficult to verify for any consumer AI music tool. This makes TuneCore a complicated path for most AI music creators right now.

## Before you upload: master the track

Every distributor has minimum audio quality requirements. More importantly, raw AI-generated tracks typically don't sound their best on streaming — and streaming platforms apply their own loudness normalization, which can make poorly mastered tracks sound worse compared to everything around them.

Suno tracks tend to export at around -13 to -14 LUFS — slightly hot for Spotify's -14 LUFS target. Treblo typically exports at around -15 to -16 LUFS, closer to target but sometimes with high True Peak values that need attention.

Before uploading to any distributor:
1. Check the integrated LUFS of your track — it should be at or below the target for your platform
2. Check True Peak — keep it at or below -1 dBTP to avoid distortion during encoding
3. Export as WAV (24-bit if possible) — don't upload an MP3 to a distributor

Drop your file into [WaveDisco Studio](https://wavedisco.com/studio) before upload — the Analyze tab reads LUFS, True Peak, and Dynamic Range in seconds, so you know exactly what needs fixing before anything goes live.

## The copyright question

This one is worth saying clearly: owning commercial rights to an AI-generated track (as granted by a platform's terms) is not the same as holding copyright in the legal sense.

In most jurisdictions — including the US — copyright typically requires human authorship. A track generated entirely by AI, without substantial human creative input, may not be eligible for copyright protection under current law. That means someone could copy your AI-generated track and you might have limited legal recourse, regardless of what the generator's TOS says about rights.

This is an evolving area. Courts and regulators in the US, EU, and UK are actively working through these questions. What's true today may look different in two years. If you're building a serious commercial project around AI-generated music, talking to a lawyer who specializes in music IP is worth the cost.

## The practical checklist

Before uploading an AI track to DistroKid or any distributor:

- [ ] Check the distributor's current AI music policy (policies change)
- [ ] Confirm you have commercial rights on the generator you used (paid tier on Suno, or verify Treblo's current terms)
- [ ] Master the track — LUFS at target, True Peak at or below -1 dBTP
- [ ] Prepare a proper cover image (3000×3000px minimum, no platform logos)
- [ ] Write accurate metadata — artist name, track title, genre, release date
- [ ] During upload: disclose AI involvement when asked — honestly
- [ ] Don't upload mass-generated content — distributors actively detect and remove it

## The short version

DistroKid is currently the most straightforward distributor for AI music from Suno or Treblo. Disclose AI involvement during upload. Make sure you have commercial rights on the generator's platform. Master the track before submitting — raw AI exports usually need loudness correction and a True Peak check. CD Baby doesn't accept AI music. TuneCore is complicated if the track is fully AI-generated.

The space is moving fast. Check current policies on the distributor's website before every release, not just the first one.
