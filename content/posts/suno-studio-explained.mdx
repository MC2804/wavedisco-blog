I almost missed that Studio 2.0 even dropped.

It came out one day after Suno announced its BMG partnership — a global licensing deal, their first with a major label, which got most of the attention that week. Studio 2.0 landed quietly the next morning and kind of disappeared into the same news cycle. I caught it a few days later and started reading through what they actually added.

There's one feature in particular that genuinely stopped me. I want to get to that. But first — there's still a lot of confusion about what Studio is, who can use it, and what you can actually do with uploaded audio depending on your plan. Let me sort that out before diving into 2.0.

---

## Who can actually use Studio

Studio is Premier-only. Pro doesn't get it. Free definitely doesn't.

This is where I see the most confusion. The table, with corrected data from Suno's current pricing page:

| Plan | Upload audio | Stems | Studio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | ✅ up to 8 min | ❌ | ❌ |
| Pro | ✅ up to 30 min | ✅ Auto + Split from mix | ❌ |
| Premier | ✅ up to 30 min | ✅ Auto + Split from mix + Advanced Split | ✅ |

A few things in here that surprised me when I checked. Free can upload audio — up to 8 minutes. I didn't expect that. And Premier's third stem mode, Advanced Split, isn't new — Suno released it separately back in June 2026. Studio 2.0 didn't create it; it brought it into the Studio interface. If you're on Pro and wondering why you only see two stem options, that's the reason.

*Current prices are on Suno's pricing page — I'm deliberately not listing them here because they change, and I'd rather you check the source than rely on a number I wrote in August.*

## "Can I upload my own track and do something with it?"

This is the question I see constantly. The honest answer: it depends on what you mean by "do something."

**Free:** You can upload a track up to 8 minutes. But you don't have stems, so you can't split it into components. And you don't have Studio, so there's no multitrack editor to bring it into. You can still use uploaded audio in Suno's standard creation workflow, but stem separation and Studio require a paid tier.

**Pro:** Upload up to 30 minutes. You get two stem modes — Auto and Split from mix — which means you can actually separate your track into parts and work with them. Still no Studio. So you can get stems out of a track you uploaded, but you're not mixing or editing in a DAW environment.

**Premier:** This is where the full thing opens up. Upload, stems (all three modes including Advanced Split), and Studio — the actual multitrack editor where you can layer, mix, and export. If someone asks "can I upload my finished track, process it, and export a clean result" — Premier is the plan where that's actually possible.

## The thing that actually surprised me in Studio 2.0

Here's the feature I mentioned. With Studio 2.0, you can import a MIDI file — or record MIDI from a controller, or type it in using your keyboard — and then **use that MIDI clip as a prompt for AI-generated audio**.

Play a chord progression. Describe what you want. Suno generates audio that follows what you played.

That's a different kind of workflow than typing a text prompt and hoping the chord structure lands right. You're deciding the harmony first, then letting the AI handle the timbre and texture. For anyone who's spent time nudging text prompts trying to get a specific chord move — this is a real change. You're not describing music anymore. You're playing it, and then asking the AI to respond.

## The rest of what 2.0 added

**Wavetable synthesizer.** Built-in, in the browser, with oscillators, filters, envelopes, LFOs, and a mod matrix. Factory presets included — basses, pads, leads, the usual. Having it inside Studio means one less tab, one less export. It's not going to replace your main soft synth, but for quick textures without leaving the session, it does the job.

**Chat bar (beta).** A text interface that reads your current session and generates things from it. Ask it for an audio clip, a MIDI part, a synth preset, or a custom effects plugin built from a description. That last one — building plugins — doesn't spend credits at launch. Suno flagged this as "may change," and I believe them, but right now it's there.

**Audio effects.** Seven factory effects, including sidechain compression and convolution reverb. You can also request additional effects through the chat bar.

**Advanced Split.** Not new — Suno released it in June 2026, before Studio 2.0. What changed in August is that Studio 2.0 brought it into the Studio interface, so you can now run it directly in the same environment where you're mixing. Premier-only.

**32-bit / 48kHz multitrack export.** A production-friendly format with plenty of headroom for continuing the mix in a full DAW.

**Technical requirements.** Desktop or tablet, minimum 768px screen, SIMD-capable CPU, at least 4GB RAM. Mobile is not supported. Web MIDI works best in Chrome.

## What Studio 2.0 is NOT

One thing that confused me when I first read the feature list: Studio has seven built-in audio effects and a chat bar that can build custom ones. That sounds like a lot of sound shaping. But Studio doesn't support external VST or Audio Units plugins — the ones you use in Ableton or Logic or your regular DAW.

If you're expecting to load a third-party reverb or compressor into Studio, it won't happen. The effects you get are the seven built-in ones plus whatever you build through the chat bar. Suno's own help documentation confirms this directly.

That's not necessarily a dealbreaker — the built-ins cover a lot of ground, and convolution reverb and sidechain compression aren't lightweight options. But if you came in expecting a plugin host, it isn't that. Better to know before you build an expectation around it.

## Studio downloads aren't subject to the regular download limit

This hasn't changed in 2.0, but it's the kind of thing that's easy to miss: exports from Studio don't count against your regular monthly download limit.

Starting September 3, Premier will include 60 standard song downloads per month. Studio multitrack exports — including the new 32-bit/48kHz ones — are tracked separately and don't pull from that quota. Suno's own blog put it plainly: Premier subscribers get multitrack export from Studio "without limitations."

So if you're doing actual production work in Studio — pulling stems, bouncing mixes, exporting multiple versions — you're not grinding through your monthly downloads to do it.

This matters a bit more now that Suno is [changing how standard downloads work on September 3](/blog/suno-september-2026-changes-explained). If Studio is your main workflow, those changes affect your standard track downloads, not your Studio exports.

---

## The honest caveat

Studio 2.0 is nine days old. The chat bar is in beta. The "no credits for plugin creation" situation is explicitly flagged by Suno as temporary.

The structural things — Studio is Premier-only, Studio exports are tracked separately from your monthly limit — those are solid and well-documented. The newer features, especially anything chat-bar-related, are where I'd verify against Suno's help pages before committing to a workflow that depends on them. Betas change.

## The short version

- Studio 2.0 launched August 13 — MIDI import/record, MIDI-as-prompt, wavetable synth, chat bar, 32-bit/48kHz export (Advanced Split existed since June 2026; Studio 2.0 brought it into the Studio interface)
- Premier-only. Pro gets stems but no Studio. Free can upload audio (up to 8 min) but no stems and no Studio
- Studio exports aren't subject to the 60/month standard download limit (which starts September 3) — Suno confirmed this directly
- The MIDI-as-prompt feature is the genuinely new thing — you set the harmony, the AI handles the sound
- Chat bar builds custom plugins without spending credits right now — Suno flagged this may change
- No VST/AU plugin support — effects are limited to the 7 built-ins plus what you build through the chat bar
- No mobile, Chrome recommended for Web MIDI
