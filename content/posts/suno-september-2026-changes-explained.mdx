On September 3, 2026, Suno is introducing download limits. If you have a library of tracks you've generated and haven't downloaded yet — you have a deadline. Here is what's actually changing, what it means by subscription tier, and why this is happening.

## What's changing, exactly

Starting September 3, downloads are capped. The limits apply to all tracks — including ones you generated before September 3. Here's what each tier gets:

| Plan | Download limit |
|---|---|
| **Free** | 7 downloads total — lifetime |
| **Pro** ($10/month) | 20 downloads per month |
| **Premier** ($30/month) | 60 downloads per month |
| **Premier + Suno Studio** | No download limit |

Additional downloads can be purchased beyond the monthly cap. The limits apply only to downloading — you can still generate as many tracks as your credit plan allows.

If you're a light user who generates a few tracks a month and downloads them as you go, this probably doesn't affect your workflow much. If you've been generating freely and letting tracks accumulate in your library without downloading — September 3 is the deadline you need to know about.

## What Suno says about why

The official language from Suno's blog talks about protecting "a healthy music ecosystem" and preventing mass export by bad actors. In [their words](https://suno.com/blog/suno-updates-tos): limiting downloads will help reduce large-scale automated abuse of the platform.

## What's actually been happening

The download limits weren't designed in a vacuum. A few things happened in the months before this announcement that provide more context.

**November 2025 — Warner Music Group settlement.** Suno reached a settlement with Warner Music Group, ending an existing copyright infringement lawsuit. The terms of that settlement included future restrictions on music downloads. The September 3 change is, in part, a contractual consequence of that deal.

**July 31, 2026 — GEMA ruling in Munich.** A court in Munich ruled against Suno in an infringement case filed by GEMA, the German music rights organization. The ruling added legal pressure on top of what was already a difficult relationship between Suno and the established music industry.

**Ongoing — UMG and Sony lawsuits.** Separate lawsuits from Universal Music Group and Sony are still working their way through the courts. The legal environment around AI music generation is under significant scrutiny right now.

The download limits, the watermarking announcement (also August 2026), and the updated Terms of Service all arrived together. That's not coincidence — it's a company trying to demonstrate good-faith cooperation with rights holders while those cases are still active.

I'm not saying the framing about "healthy ecosystems" is false. I'm saying it's incomplete. The timeline tells the fuller story.

## What the download limits actually change for most creators

Here's the realistic picture for someone who uses Suno to make music for personal projects or streaming releases.

If you generate tracks, listen to them, pick your favorites, and download those — Pro's 20 downloads a month is probably more than enough. Premier's 60 is generous for anything short of a professional production operation.

The people most affected are those who've been bulk-generating large numbers of tracks and exporting everything. The download cap makes that model significantly more expensive.

For the occasional user — the person who makes a few tracks a month and cares about quality over volume — September 3 changes very little day-to-day.

## What to do before September 3

If you have tracks in your Suno library that you haven't downloaded yet and want to keep — download them now. After September 3, those downloads will count against your monthly cap going forward.

How to do it efficiently: Suno's interface supports multi-select. On Windows, hold Ctrl and click individual tracks, or Shift+click to select a range. On Mac, use Cmd+click. Then right-click any selected track and choose **Download All** — this is built into Suno's own interface, documented in [their help center](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2409921). There's no single "export my entire library" button, so for large libraries you'll need to work through this page by page.
